# On finding a lost project

I first created this escape room project when I was just beginning to learn to code, sometime around fourteen years old and it has lived in the back of my mind ever since.
I have spent ages trying to find this and finally managed to - five years later - and I immediately ran `main.py`.
It was incredible to be plunged back into memories of my early coding journey, re-living my creation, and enjoying the creativity and chaos the past me created. I loved revisiting this project.

Then when I looked at the codebase, I cringed.
Appalling use of classes (I do not think I even knew how they worked, I just liked the syntax of creating 'dot' methods), terribly laid out text files, and a confused file structure with inconsistent naming schemes and truly awful use of whitespace.

But somehow in spite of all its obvious flaws, I was incredibly proud that even though I barely knew what I was doing back then (and even to this day), it just somehow worked, and that it was still fun to play.

If I were to recreate this project again, it would hopefully be infinitely cleaner and consistent. But it wouldn't be the same. It would lack the curiosity in programming innocence and the charm of muddled syntax which was truly necessary for me to become the programmer I am today.

I don't think I will try and reimagine this project ... at least not for a while. I will leave all the spaghetti code and the terribly nested if statements to remind me of the person I used to be, and how much I have grown. And how much has changed.

I am in university currently, studying Mathematics with plenty of programming modules. I am surrounded by people I love and care for and enjoying life to its fullest. I would love to have gone back to tell past me that everything has worked out. That even though I might not have had concrete plans for the future or grand visions of achievement, I am doing well.

I wonder how long it will be before I come back here and remember the past, who I will become. If the same person who sat typing this with tears in his eyes will come by and look at the trail of unfinished projects and half written READMEs to stop and take the time to write out his thoughts and feelings on the years gone past.

In the meantime, if anyone happens to stumble across this tiny island in the internet, have a little play. I have not touched the codebase a wink - not even to finish the half-implemented features - only touched up a few of the rooms with a few mistakes. It's not got an incredible plot or exhilarating gameplay and it's definitely filled with bugs ... but it is an earnest little project written in the early honest days of a child who wanted to make cool things.
